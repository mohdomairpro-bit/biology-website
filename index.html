Cerebral Lobes

🧠
// LOBE 01
Frontal Lobe
Controls decision-making, reasoning, planning, personality, and voluntary movement. Houses the primary motor cortex and Broca's area for speech production.
💡
// LOBE 02
Parietal Lobe
Processes sensory information — touch, temperature, pain, and spatial orientation. Houses the somatosensory cortex that maps the entire body surface.
👁️
// LOBE 03
Occipital Lobe
The brain's visual processing center. Interprets color, distance, shape, and motion. Contains the primary visual cortex at the back of the skull.
🎵
// LOBE 04
Temporal Lobe
Handles hearing, language comprehension (Wernicke's area), memory formation, and object recognition. Critical for long-term memory via the hippocampus.
⚖️
// STRUCTURE 05
Cerebellum
Coordinates muscle movement, balance, and fine motor control. Contains more neurons than the rest of the brain combined — approximately 69 billion.
🌿
// STRUCTURE 06
Brain Stem
Connects the brain to the spinal cord. Controls vital involuntary functions: breathing, heart rate, blood pressure, and sleep-wake cycles.

Internal Architecture

Cerebrum (Largest Part)

The cerebrum makes up about 85% of the brain's total weight. Divided into left and right hemispheres by the longitudinal fissure, it is connected internally by the corpus callosum — a bundle of 200–250 million nerve fibers enabling communication between hemispheres.

The outer layer, the cerebral cortex (grey matter), is 2–4mm thick and houses around 16 billion neurons. Beneath lies white matter consisting of myelinated axons.

The Neuron

Neurons are the fundamental units of the brain. Each neuron has a cell body (soma), dendrites that receive signals, and an axon that transmits electrical impulses. Myelin sheaths insulate axons and increase signal speed up to 120 m/s.

Neurons communicate at synapses using neurotransmitters such as dopamine, serotonin, and acetylcholine. This electrochemical signaling underlies all thought, emotion, and action.

Limbic System

The limbic system is the emotional and memory hub of the brain. The hippocampus converts short-term memories into long-term storage. The amygdala processes fear, anger, and emotional responses.

The hypothalamus regulates body temperature, hunger, thirst, and hormones. The thalamus acts as the brain's relay station, routing sensory information to the correct cortical areas.

The Human Eye

Sclera

The tough, white outer layer that maintains the eye's shape and provides protection. It is composed of dense collagen fibers.

Cornea

The transparent front surface of the eye. It refracts (bends) light as it enters, accounting for about 65–75% of the eye's total focusing power.

Iris & Pupil

The iris is the colored ring of muscle that controls pupil size — dilating in dim light and constricting in bright light to regulate light entry.

Lens

A flexible, transparent biconvex structure behind the iris. It fine-tunes focusing by changing shape (accommodation) to produce a sharp image on the retina.

Retina

A layer of light-sensitive photoreceptors (120 million rods for low-light vision, 6–7 million cones for color). Converts light into electrical signals.

Fovea & Macula

The fovea is the central pit of the macula, densely packed with cones. It provides the sharpest central vision and color acuity.

Optic Nerve

Carries electrical signals from retinal ganglion cells to the visual cortex in the occipital lobe. Contains ~1.2 million nerve fibers.
